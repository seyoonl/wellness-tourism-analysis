# Wellness Tourism Data Analysis

*An exploratory study of wellness tourism trends and visitor behavior in South Korea.*

**Team project** · **August 2025** · **LIKELION Data Analytics Bootcamp**

## Overview

This project explored wellness tourism in South Korea using public tourism statistics, online reviews, and inbound visitor survey data. We examined the gap between interest in wellness destinations and actual visitation, identified themes in visitor reviews, and compared travel and spending patterns across different visitor groups.

## Data & Analysis

| Area | Data / Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Domestic wellness destinations | Korea Tourism Data Lab: search interest and visitation patterns around designated wellness tourism sites |
| Visitor perceptions | Online reviews of selected wellness destinations: keyword exploration and text visualizations |
| International visitor behavior | Inbound Visitor Survey (2023–2024): comparisons of stay duration, spending, and tourism activities |
| Medical wellness focus | Group comparisons of wellness-related and dining/shopping activities, including nonparametric statistical tests |

## My Contributions

This was a four-person team project. My main responsibilities were:

- Participating in **data preprocessing and exploratory data analysis (EDA)** with the team.
- Leading the **medical wellness tourism analysis** using the 2023–2024 inbound visitor survey data.
- Defining visitor groups based on reported tourism activities and comparing **length of stay** and **travel expenditure**.
- Conducting statistical comparisons, examining effect sizes, and interpreting the results.
- Exploring combined participation in wellness and dining/shopping activities to identify differences in travel behavior.

## Selected Findings

- Wellness-related visitors tended to show **longer stays and higher total trip expenditure** than the dining/shopping-only comparison group in the exploratory analysis.
- The pattern for **daily expenditure** was not the same as for total trip expenditure, highlighting the importance of how spending is measured.
- Visitor behavior differed across groups defined by combinations of wellness-related and other tourism activities.

These are **observational associations, not evidence that wellness activities caused longer stays or higher spending**.

## Limitations

- The definition of *wellness-related tourism* was necessarily broad because of the activity categories available in the survey, and may include activities beyond medical or therapeutic services.
- Survey categories and responses may differ across years; direct comparisons require care.
- The comparisons are exploratory and do not isolate the effects of tourism activities from factors such as trip length or visitor characteristics.
